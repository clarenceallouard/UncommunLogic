## Kept for when you add a surface, deliberately unused today

`lockup-h-reverse.svg`, `lockup-v.svg`, `mark-reverse.svg`, `seal-reverse.svg`,
`motif-light-muted.svg`, `rule-ink.svg`, and the four `picto-*-reverse.svg`.

These are the opposite polarity of the files above. Reach for them the moment
you put a mark or a pictogram on the other kind of background, and do not
recolour an existing file to get there.

## The rule that decides which one

**Ink-coloured artwork goes on paper. Paper-coloured artwork goes on ink.**
The plain files are ink, the `-reverse` files are paper. Every dark section in
the CSS carries the `on-ink` class; if you are inside one of those, you want the
reverse variant.

The oxblood ring stays oxblood in both polarities. That is intentional and it
is the one tolerated use of oxblood on a dark ground, because no information
depends on reading it.

## What not to do

Do not stretch, rotate, recolour, outline or add effects to any of these. Do not
rebuild the wordmark by typing the name in Archivo: the lettering is vectorised
and the spacing and the colour of the substituted U are set file by file.
Section 06 of the brand guidelines lists the eight ways this gets broken in
practice.
