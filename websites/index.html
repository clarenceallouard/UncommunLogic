How we build

Five decisions, made once.

  1. Hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No framework unless the job genuinely needs one, and no page builder ever. A builder is fast on day one and a cage on day four hundred.
  2. No dependency you did not ask for. This site has none at all: no npm packages, no build step, no thing to update at 2am because someone else published a security advisory.
  3. Performance, accessibility and security are deliverables, not extras. Contrast measured with the WCAG formula rather than eyeballed. A content policy that forbids inline script. Keyboard operation tested. They appear in the brief with everything else.
  4. It works when things fail. Scripts blocked, motion switched off, an old browser, a slow connection: the content is still there and still readable. That is a design constraint, not a fallback.
  5. You get the repository. Source, history, credentials, documentation. Host it where you like. If you never speak to us again, it keeps working.

What it costs

Here our own rule does not apply, and we say so.

Automation has a return you can count: hours recovered, invoices chased, errors removed. We price those at one week to one month of that figure, and we show the arithmetic.

A website is different. Attributing next quarter's enquiries to a redesign is guesswork, and we are not going to dress guesswork up as a calculation to make our pricing story tidy.

So websites are quoted as a fixed price against a written brief, agreed before anything starts. You know the number, the scope, the date and what is excluded, in one document. If the scope changes, the number changes in writing first.

Where a site does have a countable return, a booking flow that removes phone calls, a quoting page that shortens the sales cycle, we cost that part the same way we cost automation, and you see both numbers separately.

Two things we will not sell you

  • A monthly retainer to keep a static site alive. It does not need one.
  • A rebuild you do not need. If your current site works and the problem is the copy, we will tell you that and the job is much smaller.

Getting found

What we actually do about search.

Included, on every build

  • One page per thing a buyer searches for, with a title and description written for that page rather than repeated across the site.
  • Structured data so search engines can read what your business is, what you offer and where you operate.
  • A sitemap, a robots file, canonical URLs, and Search Console connected so you can see what is happening.
  • Core Web Vitals treated as a build target: the page above measures three of them in front of you.
  • Real HTML. Content that only appears after JavaScript runs is content a crawler may never see.

Not included, and not offered

  • A guaranteed position. Nobody controls the ranking, and anyone selling you a position is selling you a story.
  • Bought links. They are against Google's guidelines and the penalty lands on you, not on the agency that sold them.
  • Pages written for a crawler instead of a person. They read badly, they convert badly, and Google has been discounting them for years.
  • A monthly fee for a report you will not read.
