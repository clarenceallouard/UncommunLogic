Who we are

Two people.
Both on
every job.

Uncommun Logic is Clarence Allouard and Clement Puig. There is no third person, no account manager and no build team behind us.

You meet both of us in the first hour, and the same two people write the thing. That caps how many clients we carry at once, which is exactly why the seven days holds.

Co-founder · First point of contact

Clarence Allouard

Every enquiry reaches Clarence. He runs the first hour with you, he holds the costing document, and he is the person you call when something needs to move.

His side of the build is the plumbing: automation across the tools a business already runs, the Power Platform in particular, Power Apps and Power Automate, and the unglamorous work of making systems that were never designed to speak to each other behave like one. Approvals that stop living in email. Documents generated from a form instead of typed again. A shared inbox that sorts itself. The connective tissue nobody puts in a brochure and everybody needs.

He is the reason we look at your existing licences before we look at anything new. Most of the time the capability you need is already paid for and switched off.

Co-founder · Costing and build

Clement Puig

Clement builds the costing model behind every proposal, and then builds the system. Finance training on one side, field sales and industrial automation on the other, which is an unusual combination and the reason the return calculation is the first thing we do rather than the last.

Selling to two hundred stores teaches you to measure a cycle instead of guessing at it. Working inside a factory teaches you what a production constraint actually feels like. Both show up in how we cost a process: we ask how often, how long, how many people, and what it costs when it goes wrong, because those are the four numbers that decide whether a build is worth doing.

He writes the code as well. That matters more than it sounds: the person who promised you the return is the person who has to deliver it.

Why us

Six reasons,
all of them
checkable.

01

The costing comes first

We work out what fixing your process is worth before we name a price, and we show the arithmetic. If the return is not there we say so and there is no proposal.

02

No margin on anything

No reseller agreements, no partner tiers, no referral fees. A recommendation costs us nothing to reverse, so it is worth what it says.

03

You keep the code

Source, credentials, documentation, all of it, in your accounts. If you never speak to us again the system keeps running.

04

A refusal is part of the offer

Four things we will not build, published in advance. A firm that never says no is selling capacity, not judgement.

05

Two people, deliberately

Small enough that the people who diagnosed it are the people who build it. When we are full we say we are full.

06

English and French

Both of us work in both languages, which matters more than it sounds when a European head office is involved in an Australian decision.

The name

Yes, it is
spelled that
way.

Uncommun. The O of “uncommon” was taken out and replaced with a U. The O did not disappear: it became the ring of our mark.

That mark is a NOT gate, the inversion operator of combinational logic, and the ring is its inversion bubble. So the symbol holds the letter the word is missing. It started as a typing mistake between the two of us and we kept it on purpose.

Spelling it right would be common.
